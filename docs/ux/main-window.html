Two colours mean something. One hue carries the rest.

Red — live: on air, rolling, and the button that goes live
Green — next up, in preview
Amber — everything else the panel says
Grey — dead or offline

Red and green are the oldest signal in a gallery and there is no reason to give them up, so they are here — and reserved. Red means one thing: live — a picture on air, a stream rolling to disk, the button that starts either. Green means one thing: next. Nothing else may be either colour — not a healthy node, not a passing check. Health, counters, warnings and every label run on the single amber hue, which is what keeps a red cell readable at a glance across ninety-six of them.

Why it is shaped this way

1

Spread sits in the status bar

Not "in sync yes/no" but by how much. Camera crystals drift roughly 36 ms an hour, so this number grows during a show and has to stay in sight.

2

Preview → Take → Program

The target camera warms up in preview first; Take then cross-fades an already running picture. This is not comfort — it is the only way to get a transition on four paths to land on the same instant.

Rejected: cutting straight to air
3

One lens, switchable

Tiles show proxy streams from the nodes, one lens per camera. The selector above changes which of the four — for every camera at once.

Rejected: a quad of all four lenses
4

Desk runs at full quality

Multiview runs on small proxies. The two cameras being decided on come from the full stream — you cannot judge focus on a proxy.

5

Delay is within reach, not in settings

It belongs to the camera in preview and gets touched during a show, not during the build. So it sits beside the desk.

6

Twelve nodes have to fit

Chips instead of rows. One number matters mid-show — how much longer the worst one can record — and it is spelled out underneath. N04 records but serves no proxy.

7

The chain has a new shape

Cameras publish to their own nodes, not to the Mac. Only two cameras cross the Mac — which is why it reads "2 dec · 4 enc" and not 96 streams.

Rejected: routing everything through the Mac
8

The T-bar is the same transition, driven by hand

Take runs the dissolve on a timer. The bar next to it runs the same dissolve on your wrist — hold it half way, ease it in, stop when the moment is not there yet. Underneath it is one number between 0 and 1 either way, so the bar costs nothing extra. It binds to a physical fader on the MIDI desk.

9

Warnings are amber, not red

A node running out of disk blinks amber; it never goes red. Red is spent entirely on what is live, so it keeps meaning that. It is the difference between glancing at the panel and having to read it.
